# Research decisions

## D1 — Preserve the full research objective

**Decision:** Treat the attachment as a complete research programme, not merely a request for a repository or proposal.

**Reason:** Mathematical validity and a proved contribution are the primary success criteria; engineering artifacts are supporting evidence.

## D2 — Literature before conjecture

**Decision:** Search primary literature and inspect mathematical statements before selecting a flagship problem.

**Reason:** The directive explicitly prohibits invented gaps and unsupported novelty claims.

## D3 — Parallel evidence gathering

**Decision:** Commission five separate literature searches covering materially different equation families.

**Reason:** This avoids committing to additive-only stability and allows an evidence-based candidate matrix.

## D4 — Honest current status

**Decision:** Record all major deliverables as incomplete until independently checked.

**Reason:** No theorem, manuscript, formal proof, or remote publication exists yet.

## D5 — Abandon C1 as an original flagship

**Decision:** Retain the finite-order `ε/2` theorem only as verified background; do not claim it as an original contribution.

**Reason:** Forti (2019), Theorem 1.5, already implies the bound by scalarization. Rätz (2003) and Popa–Rus (2026) provide closely related exact and sharp results. Mathematical correctness does not compensate for lack of novelty.

## D6 — Re-evaluate four substantive alternatives

**Decision:** Investigate the Pexider quadratic cone theorem, critical `p`-adic Jensen stability, Pexiderized Fréchet constants, and general valued-field dilation in parallel.

**Reason:** These directions have identifiable mathematical content and were not eliminated by the C1 audit. Each must still pass proof and novelty checks.

## D7 — Select normalized sharp Pexider stability

**Decision:** Select C2N as the flagship theorem.

**Reason:** The exact normalized vector `(ε/2, ε)` appears new in the audited literature and substantially improves Yang's `(2ε, 3ε)` bounds. The proof is complete, both constants are sharp, and the central result, exact structure, sharpness, and uniqueness are Lean-verified.
